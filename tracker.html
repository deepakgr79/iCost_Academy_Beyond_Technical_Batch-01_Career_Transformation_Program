Daily & periodic minimums

Tracked per person, every day of the 90
Original posts
1–2 / day
At least one self-written LinkedIn post daily. Two is the cap that counts.
Long-form article
1 / 10 days
One published LinkedIn article minimum inside every rolling 10-day block.
Comments given
5 / day
Thoughtful comments on five different posts — outside the cohort.
Reposts / shares
2 / day
Amplify two posts from your feed — outside the cohort.
New connections
5 / day
Five new connection requests sent to your extended network.
External network only. Comments, reposts and connection requests count only when directed outside the cohort. Engaging with a fellow Batch 01 participant's post does not satisfy any of these minimums — the point of the exercise is to build reach into each person's own network, not to recycle activity inside the room.

How compliance is scored — for each program day elapsed, a day counts as fully compliant when that day's log shows 1–2 posts, ≥5 external comments, ≥2 external reposts and ≥5 external connection requests. Compliance % = fully-compliant days ÷ days elapsed. Article cadence is tracked separately against the 1-per-10-days floor and doesn't affect the daily score. A dash means no log has been received yet.
Admin — how this stays up to date

Every participant logs through the “Log today's activity” page above — a plain page, no Google sign-in, no Google Forms screen ever shown. Behind the scenes it writes into a private Google Sheet, invisibly, the moment someone submits.

This leaderboard's numbers are not pulled live from that sheet automatically — the sheet can't be fetched automatically from here. To refresh the standings, open this conversation and ask to pull the latest numbers; the sheet gets re-read and this page gets republished with updated figures. Do that as often as you'd like — weekly is plenty for a 90-day program.

The intake sheet lives in the Google account it was set up under. Nobody needs to open the sheet or the underlying form directly — this page and the log page are the only two surfaces anyone should ever use.
